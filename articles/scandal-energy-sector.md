# SCANDAL IN THE ENERGY SECTOR: GH¢8.8 BILLION HOLE EXPOSED
## ECG and GNPC Flout Rules, Fueling Ghana’s Fiscal Crisis. A Call for Surcharge and Prosecution
**By Adam Ibrahim**

The 2023 Auditor-General's Report on Public Boards, Corporations, and Other Statutory Institutions is a stark warning to the nation, revealing systemic corruption and gross negligence within state enterprises. While the total irregularities across all public boards amounted to a staggering **GH¢8.8 billion**, the bulk of the most egregious breaches were concentrated in the energy sector.

---

### 1. ECG: The Procurement Chaos and Metering Mess
The Electricity Company of Ghana (ECG) is flagged for alarming non-compliance with the **Public Procurement Act (Act 663)**. 

#### The Single-Sourcing Surge
The most damning finding is the explosion of single-source contracts. 
* **512 contracts** awarded through single-sourcing in 2023.
* This represents **72.83%** of all contracts awarded that year.

#### The $145 Million Value-for-Money Failure
ECG awarded contracts worth over **US$145 million** for more than 800,000 meters without properly seeking value for money. This contributes to:
1. High commercial losses due to meter shortages.
2. Inflated costs passed on to consumers through tariffs.

> **DEMAND FOR INVESTIGATION:** The Head of Procurement and the Board of ECG must be held accountable for circumventing the Public Procurement Act.

---

### 2. GNPC: Misapplied Funds and Stalled Projects
The Ghana National Petroleum Corporation (GNPC) is accused of prioritizing unapproved projects over critical national assignments.

* **GH¢4.9 Million for Unapproved Projects:** Funds were paid for the Royal Golf Club House without Parliamentary approval.
* **GH¢2 Million Wastage:** The GNPC Foundation spent millions on CSI projects that have since deteriorated due to lack of planning.

---

### Conclusion: Accountability Must Be Enforced
Parliament's Public Accounts Committee (PAC) must enforce **Disallowance and Surcharge** provisions. Officers responsible must personally refund the state's losses. 

Failure to act will signal that fiscal impunity remains the status quo.
